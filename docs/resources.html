Doing the same in JASP

Result JASP
t, p, 95% CI, Cohen’s d T-Tests → Independent Samples T-Test (enjoyment by condition); tick Location parameter and Effect size
BF₁₀, prior and posterior for δ T-Tests → Bayesian Independent Samples T-Test; Prior and posterior, Additional info
Robustness check (Scene 2) same analysis → Bayes factor robustness check
Informed priors (Scene 2) same analysis → Prior → Informed → Normal (location 0.3, scale 0.15)
Equivalence test (Scene 3) Equivalence T-Tests module → Independent Samples; bounds ±0.3 (raw)
Sequential updating (Scene 4) not a single menu; use the Bayesian t-test’s Sequential analysis for one study

Where to go next

  • Lakens (2022), Improving Your Statistical Inferences (free online), especially the chapters on p values, likelihoods, and Bayesian statistics, which walk through common misconceptions about Bayes factors.
  • Kristoffer Magnusson’s interactive visualizations: Bayesian inference and confidence intervals.
  • Bayes Rules! (Johnson, Ott, & Dogucu, 2022), free at bayesrulesbook.com, for a full course in applied Bayesian modeling in R.
  • bayesEd (Papenberg), an R package that visualizes the Bayesian t-test as a prediction contest; Scene 2 follows the same logic.
  • Informative hypotheses such as “enjoyment: rewarded > no consequence > punished” can be tested directly with Bayes factors (Hoijtink et al., 2019; R package bain).
  • In communication: Konijn et al. (2015) reanalyze media-violence and mediated-communication datasets with Bayes factors and argue this can reduce publication bias. Levine et al. (2008) critique NHST practice in quantitative communication research.

- ***Bayes Rules!*** (Johnson, Ott, & Dogucu, 2022), free at [bayesrulesbook.com](https://www.bayesrulesbook.com/), for a full course in applied Bayesian modeling in R.
- **[bayesEd](https://github.com/m-Py/bayesEd)** (Papenberg), an R package that visualizes the Bayesian *t*-test as a prediction contest; [Scene 2](scene-2.qmd) follows the same logic.
- **Informative hypotheses** such as "enjoyment: rewarded > no consequence > punished" can be tested directly with Bayes factors (Hoijtink et al., 2019; R package `bain`).
- **In communication:** Konijn et al. (2015) reanalyze media-violence and mediated-communication datasets with Bayes factors and argue this can reduce publication bias. Levine et al. (2008) critique NHST practice in quantitative communication research.
